# Hi, I'm Shaun

I work in B2B sales as a Solutions Consultant at AiCore, a UK apprenticeship provider that trains companies' teams to use AI in practical ways. Here I share personal projects that show how AI can make everyday work clearer, faster and easier to check. The examples are fictional or have had real details removed. I share what worked, what didn't, and what I'd change.

## Featured Projects

**[Practical AI Sales Workflows](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/practical-ai-sales-workflows).** AI workflows for everyday B2B sales work, from call prep to follow-up, objections and keeping a CRM honest, each with a one-page recipe card. The [evidence-status matrix](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/practical-ai-sales-workflows/blob/main/EVIDENCE-STATUS.md) shows what has been tested and how. No independent use has been logged yet.

**[Practical AI Adoption](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/practical-ai-adoption).** Plain-English guides for using AI at work without a technical background, starting with writing a better brief and checking what comes back.

**[Say It Plainly](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/say-it-plainly).** Five skills that review or rewrite your writing in plain words, plus a [private checker](https://shaunmarsden.github.io/say-it-plainly/) that runs in your browser. In my small tests, a one-paragraph instruction kept a draft's claims as well as a skill did, and on longer drafts both did better than a bare request.

**[Find My Next Job](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/find-my-next-job)** and **[Find Freelance Work](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/find-freelance-work).** Paste a skill into any AI chat with your experience, your limits and the adverts or projects you've found, and it says which are worth applying for, which need a question first and which to skip. In my small tests with made-up jobseekers, the job skill sorted 33 of 33 adverts correctly against 29 with no skill. For freelance work, a five-line prompt did at least as well as the full skill, so start with that.

**[Commercial Career Pivot Workbench](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/commercial-career-pivot-workbench).** A workflow for sales and commercial people thinking about a career change, checking the evidence at each step.

## A Few Rules I Try to Follow

- Start with a real problem, not a tool looking for a use case
- Keep facts, estimates and assumptions clearly separate
- Keep a person responsible for the final decision
- Don't put sensitive information into tools that aren't approved
- Publish the honest score, not just the polished result

## Also Worth Knowing About

Not mine, but useful:

- [Wispr Flow Media Pause](https://github.com/kierzio/wispr-flow-media-pause) pauses whatever you're playing while you dictate with Wispr Flow, for USB audio interfaces macOS can't mute.
- [macos-mic-keepwarm](https://github.com/drewburchfield/macos-mic-keepwarm) keeps the microphone awake, so dictation apps start listening at once instead of losing the first half-second.
- [Premortem](https://gist.github.com/oscheers/9d722938ce35dab2a07832e69b688871), a Claude skill by Ollie Scheers, assumes a project has already failed and works back to why, sorting the causes into real risks, overblown ones and the ones nobody says out loud.
